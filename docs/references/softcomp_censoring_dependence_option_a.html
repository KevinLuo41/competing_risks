Internal note · Response to Thomas's comments on the loss

SoftComp's training loss depends on the censoring distributionOption A: treat censoring as one more softmax state

None of the three current training terms targets the CIF at the population level, and all of them shift with the censoring distribution G. Option A adds censoring as an extra class, so every subject has an observed label at every time point. The training target then no longer depends on G, and the CIF is recovered through the Aalen–Johansen identity.

Current algorithm and loss

Data and target

We observe nn independent samples

{(xi,Yi,Δi)}i=1n,Yi=min(Ti,Ci),Δi=ϵi𝟙(Ti≤Ci)∈{0,1,…,K}.\{(x_i,\,Y_i,\,\Delta_i)\}_{i=1}^{n},\qquad Y_i=\min(T_i,C_i),\qquad \Delta_i=\epsilon_i\,𝟙(T_i\le C_i)\in\{0,1,\dots,K\}.

Δi=0\Delta_i=0 denotes censoring and Δi=k\Delta_i=k an event of cause kk; CC is assumed independent of TT given xx. The targets are the cause-specific CIFs and the overall survival function:

Fk(t∣x)=P(T≤t,ϵ=k∣X=x),S(t∣x)=1−∑k=1KFk(t∣x).F_k(t\mid x)=P(T\le t,\ \epsilon=k\mid X=x),\qquad S(t\mid x)=1-\sum_{k=1}^{K}F_k(t\mid x).

Model: a (K+1)(K+1)-class softmax at every time tt

Each cause gets a logit in (x,t)(x,t), the log-odds of its CIF relative to survival:

logFk(t∣x)S(t∣x)=μk(x,t),k=1,…,K.\log\frac{F_k(t\mid x)}{S(t\mid x)}=\mu_k(x,t),\qquad k=1,\dots,K.

Solving for FkF_k and SS gives a softmax over K+1K+1 states (survival plus KK causes) at each time tt, with the survival logit fixed at 0:

Fk(t∣x)=eμk(x,t)1+∑j=1Keμj(x,t),S(t∣x)=11+∑j=1Keμj(x,t).F_k(t\mid x)=\frac{e^{\mu_k(x,t)}}{1+\sum_{j=1}^{K}e^{\mu_j(x,t)}},\qquad S(t\mid x)=\frac{1}{1+\sum_{j=1}^{K}e^{\mu_j(x,t)}}.

Hence ∑kFk+S=1\sum_k F_k+S=1 and Fk≥0F_k\ge0 hold automatically, but monotonicity of FkF_k in tt is not guaranteed. Below, π0=S\pi_0=S and πk=Fk\pi_k=F_k.

Network: a residual feed-forward network with input [x;t][x;t]

h0=ReLU(W0[x;t]+b0)∈ℝd,hℓ=hℓ−1+Wℓ,2ReLU(Wℓ,1hℓ−1+bℓ,1)+bℓ,2,ℓ=1,…,L,μ(x,t)=WL+1hL+bL+1∈ℝK.\begin{aligned} h_0&=\mathrm{ReLU}\left(W_0[x;t]+b_0\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\ h_\ell&=h_{\ell-1}+W_{\ell,2}\,\mathrm{ReLU}\left(W_{\ell,1}h_{\ell-1}+b_{\ell,1}\right)+b_{\ell,2},\qquad \ell=1,\dots,L,\\ \mu(x,t)&=W_{L+1}h_L+b_{L+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}. \end{aligned}

The experiments use d=32d=32 and L=1L=1 (fewer than 3,000 parameters); time tt enters on its raw scale (CRSoftNet.forward, crsoft.py:488-494).

Loss ① · Eq. (5): (K+1)(K+1)-class cross-entropy at the observed time YiY_i

ℓ1(xi,Yi,Δi)=−logπΔi(Yi∣xi)={−logFk(Yi∣xi),Δi=k≥1,−logS(Yi∣xi),Δi=0.\ell_1(x_i,Y_i,\Delta_i)=-\log\pi_{\Delta_i}(Y_i\mid x_i)=\begin{cases}-\log F_k(Y_i\mid x_i), & \Delta_i=k\ge1,\\ -\log S(Y_i\mid x_i), & \Delta_i=0.\end{cases}

The paper calls this the multinomial negative log-likelihood (competing_risks_loss, crsoft.py:16-54). For comparison, the actual censored-data likelihood is

−logLi=−∑k=1K𝟙(Δi=k)logfk(Yi∣xi)−𝟙(Δi=0)logS(Yi∣xi),fk=∂Fk∂t.-\log L_i=-\sum_{k=1}^{K}𝟙(\Delta_i=k)\log f_k(Y_i\mid x_i)-𝟙(\Delta_i=0)\log S(Y_i\mid x_i),\qquad f_k=\frac{\partial F_k}{\partial t}.

The difference: events use the cumulative probability Fk(Yi)F_k(Y_i), not the density fk(Yi)f_k(Y_i)

Loss ② · Time augmentation

In every minibatch, MM times ti1,…,tiM∼iidUnif(0,Yi)t_{i1},\dots,t_{iM}\overset{\text{iid}}{\sim}\mathrm{Unif}(0,Y_i) are redrawn for each sample. All of them are labeled as survival (0), each with weight λaug\lambda_{\text{aug}} (written λ\lambda in the paper):

ℒ=1n∑i=1nℓ1(xi,Yi,Δi)+λaug∑j=1M[−logS(tij∣xi)]1+Mλaug.ℒ=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\ell_1(x_i,Y_i,\Delta_i)+\lambda_{\text{aug}}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\left[-\log S(t_{ij}\mid x_i)\right]}{1+M\lambda_{\text{aug}}}.

Settings: M=2M=2, λaug=0.5\lambda_{\text{aug}}=0.5 (Cases II/III, PBC, Framingham); M=8M=8, λaug=0.5\lambda_{\text{aug}}=0.5 (Synthetic). Case I uses no augmentation.

Loss ③ · Eq. (6): auxiliary Brier term (PBC and Synthetic only)

ℒBrier(xi,Yi,Δi)=1M′K∑m=1M′∑k=1K[Fk(t′im∣xi)−𝟙{Yi≤t′im,Δi=k}]2,t′im∼Unif(0,tmax),ℒ_{\text{Brier}}(x_i,Y_i,\Delta_i)=\frac{1}{M'K}\sum_{m=1}^{M'}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left[F_k(t'_{im}\mid x_i)-𝟙\{Y_i\le t'_{im},\ \Delta_i=k\}\right]^2,\qquad t'_{im}\sim\mathrm{Unif}(0,t_{\max}),

where tmax=maxiYit_{\max}=\max_i Y_i, with no IPCW weighting (_brier_loss, crsoft.py:96-121). The full training objective is

minθℒ+λB⋅1n∑i=1nℒBrier(xi,Yi,Δi),\min_{\theta}\ \ ℒ+\lambda_B\cdot\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}ℒ_{\text{Brier}}(x_i,Y_i,\Delta_i),

with λB=2\lambda_B=2, M′=10M'=10 for PBC; λB=5\lambda_B=5, M′=5M'=5 for Synthetic; and λB=0\lambda_B=0 for all other datasets.

The problem: what these losses learn at the population level

Population minimizers of the three training terms

Let λk(t∣x)\lambda_k(t\mid x) be the cause-specific hazard, λ=∑kλk\lambda=\sum_k\lambda_k, λc(t∣x)\lambda_c(t\mid x) the censoring hazard, and G(t∣x)=P(C>t∣x)G(t\mid x)=P(C>t\mid x). When the network is flexible enough in tt, each risk can be minimized pointwise in (x,t)(x,t) (Gibbs’ inequality / conditional expectation), which gives:

① Eq. (5) learns the probability of which outcome is observed, given the observed time. This contains the censoring hazard; without censoring, S≡0S\equiv0.

πk*(t∣x)=λk(t∣x)λ(t∣x)+λc(t∣x)=P(Δ=k∣Y=t,X=x)\pi_k^*(t\mid x)=\frac{\lambda_k(t\mid x)}{\lambda(t\mid x)+\lambda_c(t\mid x)}=P(\Delta=k\mid Y=t,\,X=x)

② Adding augmentation puts an extra “pseudo-hazard” in the denominator. It suppresses the early overestimation, but it also pulls the whole CIF down and depends on MM, λaug\lambda_{\text{aug}}, and λc\lambda_c.

πk*(t∣x)=λk(t∣x)λ(t∣x)+λc(t∣x)+λaugM𝔼[1/Y∣Y>t,X=x]\pi_k^*(t\mid x)=\frac{\lambda_k(t\mid x)}{\lambda(t\mid x)+\lambda_c(t\mid x)+\lambda_{\text{aug}}M\,\mathbb{E}\left[1/Y\mid Y>t,\,X=x\right]}

③ Eq. (6) learns the observed sub-distribution, which is biased downward, more so under heavier censoring.

πk*(t∣x)=P(Y≤t,Δ=k∣x)=∫0tG(s∣x)dFk(s∣x)≤Fk(t∣x)\pi_k^*(t\mid x)=P(Y\le t,\,\Delta=k\mid x)=\int_0^t G(s\mid x)\,dF_k(s\mid x)\ \le\ F_k(t\mid x)

None of these is Fk(t∣x)F_k(t\mid x), and all of them change with GG

03

What Option A changes

Idea Munch & Gerds 2026

Treat censoring as a state of its own. Then the observed state ηi(t)\eta_i(t) of every subject is known at every time tt, and classifying this state with a softmax is a fully observed, strictly proper problem that does not require estimating GG. The CIF is then recovered from the observed-state probabilities through the Aalen–Johansen identity. Manuscript III of Pham’s (2024) PhD thesis (Pham, Munch & Gerds) follows the same idea of modeling censoring jointly as an extra cause.

Observed-state process: the new labels

For each sample, define the observed-state process. It is fully determined by (Yi,Δi)(Y_i,\Delta_i), and hence known for every t∈[0,τ]t\in[0,\tau]:

ηi(t)={0,t<Yi,k,t≥Yi,Δi=k≥1,c,t≥Yi,Δi=0.\eta_i(t)=\begin{cases}0, & t<Y_i,\\ k, & t\ge Y_i,\ \Delta_i=k\ge1,\\ c, & t\ge Y_i,\ \Delta_i=0.\end{cases}

Here 00 means at risk and uncensored, kk means an event of cause kk has been observed, and cc means censored. The state probabilities are

P0(t∣x)=P(Y>t∣x),Pk(t∣x)=P(Y≤t,Δ=k∣x),Pc(t∣x)=P(Y≤t,Δ=0∣x).P_0(t\mid x)=P(Y>t\mid x),\qquad P_k(t\mid x)=P(Y\le t,\ \Delta=k\mid x),\qquad P_c(t\mid x)=P(Y\le t,\ \Delta=0\mid x).

Assuming censoring is conditionally independent given XX and P0(τ∣x)>0P_0(\tau\mid x)>0, they relate to the target FkF_k and the censoring distribution GG by (Munch & Gerds, Eq. 7)

P0(t∣x)=S(t∣x)G(t∣x),Pk(t∣x)=∫0tG(s−∣x)dFk(s∣x),Pc(t∣x)=−∫0tS(s∣x)dG(s∣x).P_0(t\mid x)=S(t\mid x)\,G(t\mid x),\qquad P_k(t\mid x)=\int_0^t G(s^-\mid x)\,dF_k(s\mid x),\qquad P_c(t\mid x)=-\int_0^t S(s\mid x)\,dG(s\mid x).

It does not matter that these quantities depend on GG: GG cancels when the CIF is recovered (see the AJ identity below).

Model: a (K+2)(K+2)-class softmax at every time tt

The network is identical to Section 01, except that the output layer has K+1K+1 instead of KK dimensions (one extra censoring logit):

ν(x,t)=(ν1(x,t),…,νK(x,t),νc(x,t))⊤=WL+1hL+bL+1∈ℝK+1.\nu(x,t)=\left(\nu_1(x,t),\dots,\nu_K(x,t),\nu_c(x,t)\right)^\top=W_{L+1}h_L+b_{L+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{K+1}.

With the at-risk logit fixed at 0, the observed-state probabilities are

P0(t∣x)=1D(x,t),Pk(t∣x)=eνk(x,t)D(x,t),Pc(t∣x)=eνc(x,t)D(x,t),D(x,t)=1+∑j=1Keνj(x,t)+eνc(x,t).P_0(t\mid x)=\frac{1}{D(x,t)},\qquad P_k(t\mid x)=\frac{e^{\nu_k(x,t)}}{D(x,t)},\qquad P_c(t\mid x)=\frac{e^{\nu_c(x,t)}}{D(x,t)},\qquad D(x,t)=1+\sum_{j=1}^{K}e^{\nu_j(x,t)}+e^{\nu_c(x,t)}.

The network now outputs the observed-state probabilities PP rather than FkF_k; FkF_k comes from the AJ recursion below.

Loss: integrated cross-entropy over the observed state

In every minibatch, draw MM times ti1,…,tiMt_{i1},\dots,t_{iM} for each sample independently from a fixed distribution ww on [0,τ][0,\tau] (independent of YiY_i), and label them by ηi(t)\eta_i(t):

ℓA(xi,Yi,Δi)=1M∑m=1M[−logPηi(tim)(tim∣xi)],\ell_A(x_i,Y_i,\Delta_i)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left[-\log P_{\eta_i(t_{im})}(t_{im}\mid x_i)\right],

−logPηi(t)(t∣xi)={−logP0(t∣xi),t<Yi,−logPk(t∣xi),t≥Yi,Δi=k≥1,−logPc(t∣xi),t≥Yi,Δi=0.-\log P_{\eta_i(t)}(t\mid x_i)=\begin{cases}-\log P_0(t\mid x_i), & t<Y_i,\\ -\log P_k(t\mid x_i), & t\ge Y_i,\ \Delta_i=k\ge1,\\ -\log P_c(t\mid x_i), & t\ge Y_i,\ \Delta_i=0.\end{cases}

The training objective is the sample average, i.e., a Monte Carlo approximation of the integral ∫0τ(⋅)w(dt)\int_0^\tau(\cdot)\,w(dt):

minθℒA=1n∑i=1nℓA(xi,Yi,Δi)≈1n∑i=1n∫0τ−logPηi(t)(t∣xi)w(dt).\min_\theta\ \ ℒ_A=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell_A(x_i,Y_i,\Delta_i)\ \approx\ \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_0^\tau-\log P_{\eta_i(t)}(t\mid x_i)\,w(dt).

  • Choice of ww: for example Unif(0,τ)\mathrm{Unif}(0,\tau), with τ\tau the upper end of the evaluation window (such as the 90%–97.5% quantile of observed times). Alternatively, the empirical distribution of the observed times YY puts more weight where data are dense.
  • One minibatch: draw tim∼wt_{im}\sim w; label 0 if tim<Yit_{im}<Y_i, otherwise Δi\Delta_i (class cc if censored); feed (xi,tim)(x_i,t_{im}) through the network to get K+1K+1 logits, prepend 0, apply log-softmax, pick the labeled entry, and average.
  • Compared with the current loss: the single-point ℓ1\ell_1 at YiY_i, λaug\lambda_{\text{aug}}, and the auxiliary Brier term are gone; the new ingredient is the label for t≥Yit\ge Y_i (event kk or censored cc).

Why this does not depend on GG: for every tt,

𝔼[−logPη(t)(t∣X)∣X=x]=−∑l∈{0,1,…,K,c}Pltrue(t∣x)logPl(t∣x)≥−∑lPltrue(t∣x)logPltrue(t∣x),\mathbb{E}\left[-\log P_{\eta(t)}(t\mid X)\mid X=x\right]=-\sum_{l\in\{0,1,\dots,K,c\}}P^{\text{true}}_l(t\mid x)\log P_l(t\mid x)\ \ge\ -\sum_{l}P^{\text{true}}_l(t\mid x)\log P^{\text{true}}_l(t\mid x),

with equality if and only if Pl=PltrueP_l=P^{\text{true}}_l (Gibbs’ inequality). The minimizer is therefore the true observed-state probability, whatever ww and MM are. All labels are observed, so GG is never needed during training. The Brier version 1n∑i∫0τ∑l[Pl(t∣xi)−𝟙{ηi(t)=l}]2w(dt)\frac{1}{n}\sum_i\int_0^\tau\sum_l\left[P_l(t\mid x_i)-𝟙\{\eta_i(t)=l\}\right]^2w(dt), which is Munch & Gerds’ B‾τ\bar{B}_\tau, works as well.

Recovering the CIF: the Aalen–Johansen identity

On a grid 0=s0<s1<…<sJ=τ0=s_0<s_1<\dots<s_J=\tau, start from S(s0)=1S(s_0)=1 and Fk(s0)=0F_k(s_0)=0 and recurse:

ΔΛk(sj)=[Pk(sj)−Pk(sj−1)]+P0(sj−1),Fk(sj)=Fk(sj−1)+S(sj−1)ΔΛk(sj),S(sj)=S(sj−1)(1−∑kΔΛk(sj)).\begin{aligned} \Delta\Lambda_k(s_j)&=\frac{\left[P_k(s_j)-P_k(s_{j-1})\right]_+}{P_0(s_{j-1})},\\ F_k(s_j)&=F_k(s_{j-1})+S(s_{j-1})\,\Delta\Lambda_k(s_j),\\ S(s_j)&=S(s_{j-1})\left(1-\sum_{k}\Delta\Lambda_k(s_j)\right). \end{aligned}

This corresponds to Eqs. (3) and (11) in Munch & Gerds. The resulting CIFs are monotone by construction and satisfy S+∑kFk=1S+\sum_kF_k=1, so PAV and rescaling are no longer needed. The same recursion also yields the censoring distribution Ĝ(t∣x)\hat G(t\mid x).

Code changes (about 100–150 lines, mostly in crsoft.py)

  • The output layer goes from KK to K+1K+1 dimensions (one extra censoring class).
  • _train_step: the three loss terms collapse into a single (K+2)(K+2)-class cross-entropy, which is shorter than the current code.
  • predict_cif_survival_grid: compute PP on a fine grid, then run the AJ recursion above; the return format is unchanged.
  • The experiment scripts keep the same interface; only aug_weight and brier_lambda are dropped.

Most of the work is re-running all SoftComp results (the baselines stay as they are), adding a censoring-level comparison, and rewriting Section 2.4 of the paper. Risks: increment errors are amplified in the tail where P0P_0 is small, and the C-index advantage may change, which has to be checked experimentally.
