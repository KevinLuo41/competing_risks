The new model JointSoftComp: name, model, and loss

Why “JointSoftComp”

The joint survival super learner of Munch & Gerds (2026) builds a competing risks model for the observed data in which censoring is a state of its own, so that at every time point it is known which state each individual is in. The event-time distribution and the censoring distribution are thereby described and evaluated jointly through a single observed-state process. JointSoftComp keeps SoftComp's softmax structure and only changes what the softmax is over: one network outputs the probabilities of the states “at risk / KK causes / censored”, and the CIF is recovered through Eqs. (3) and (11) of that paper. Hence the name: Joint survival + SoftComp.

Data and target

We observe nn independent samples

{(xi,Yi,Δi)}i=1n,Yi=min(Ti,Ci),Δi=ϵi𝟙(Ti≤Ci)∈{0,1,…,K}.\{(x_i,\,Y_i,\,\Delta_i)\}_{i=1}^{n},\qquad Y_i=\min(T_i,C_i),\qquad \Delta_i=\epsilon_i\,𝟙(T_i\le C_i)\in\{0,1,\dots,K\}.

Δi=0\Delta_i=0 denotes censoring and Δi=k\Delta_i=k an event of cause kk; CC is assumed independent of TT given xx. The targets are the cause-specific CIFs and the overall survival function:

Fk(t∣x)=P(T≤t,ϵ=k∣X=x),S(t∣x)=1−∑k=1KFk(t∣x).F_k(t\mid x)=P(T\le t,\ \epsilon=k\mid X=x),\qquad S(t\mid x)=1-\sum_{k=1}^{K}F_k(t\mid x).

Observed-state process: an observed label at every time

For each sample, define the observed-state process. It is fully determined by (Yi,Δi)(Y_i,\Delta_i) and hence known at every time tt:

ηi(t)={0,t<Yi,k,t≥Yi,Δi=k≥1,c,t≥Yi,Δi=0.\eta_i(t)=\begin{cases}0, & t<Y_i,\\ k, & t\ge Y_i,\ \Delta_i=k\ge1,\\ c, & t\ge Y_i,\ \Delta_i=0.\end{cases}

Here 00 means at risk and uncensored, kk that an event of cause kk has been observed, and cc that the subject has been censored. The state probabilities (Munch & Gerds' F(t,l,x)F(t,l,x)) are

P0(t∣x)=P(Y>t∣x),Pk(t∣x)=P(Y≤t,Δ=k∣x),Pc(t∣x)=P(Y≤t,Δ=0∣x).P_0(t\mid x)=P(Y>t\mid x),\qquad P_k(t\mid x)=P(Y\le t,\ \Delta=k\mid x),\qquad P_c(t\mid x)=P(Y\le t,\ \Delta=0\mid x).

Under conditionally independent censoring and P0(τ∣x)>0P_0(\tau\mid x)>0, they relate to the target FkF_k and to the censoring distribution G(t∣x)=P(C>t∣x)G(t\mid x)=P(C>t\mid x) by (Munch & Gerds, Eq. 7)

P0=SG,Pk(t∣x)=∫0tG(s−∣x)dFk(s∣x),Pc(t∣x)=−∫0tS(s∣x)dG(s∣x).P_0=S\,G,\qquad P_k(t\mid x)=\int_0^t G(s^-\mid x)\,dF_k(s\mid x),\qquad P_c(t\mid x)=-\int_0^t S(s\mid x)\,dG(s\mid x).

That PP depends on GG does not matter: GG cancels when the CIF is recovered (see the AJ identity below).

Model: a (K+2)(K+2)-class softmax at every time tt

The network is the same residual feed-forward network with input [x;t][x;t] as in SoftComp (manuscript); the output layer has one extra censoring logit:

h0=ReLU(W0[x;t]+b0)∈ℝd,hℓ=hℓ−1+Wℓ,2ReLU(Wℓ,1hℓ−1+bℓ,1)+bℓ,2,ℓ=1,…,L,ν(x,t)=(ν1,…,νK,νc)⊤=WL+1hL+bL+1∈ℝK+1.\begin{aligned} h_0&=\mathrm{ReLU}\left(W_0[x;t]+b_0\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\ h_\ell&=h_{\ell-1}+W_{\ell,2}\,\mathrm{ReLU}\left(W_{\ell,1}h_{\ell-1}+b_{\ell,1}\right)+b_{\ell,2},\qquad \ell=1,\dots,L,\\ \nu(x,t)&=\left(\nu_1,\dots,\nu_K,\nu_c\right)^\top=W_{L+1}h_L+b_{L+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{K+1}. \end{aligned}

With the at-risk logit fixed at 0, the observed-state probabilities are

P0(t∣x)=1D,Pk(t∣x)=eνk(x,t)D,Pc(t∣x)=eνc(x,t)D,D=1+∑j=1Keνj(x,t)+eνc(x,t).P_0(t\mid x)=\frac{1}{D},\qquad P_k(t\mid x)=\frac{e^{\nu_k(x,t)}}{D},\qquad P_c(t\mid x)=\frac{e^{\nu_c(x,t)}}{D},\qquad D=1+\sum_{j=1}^{K}e^{\nu_j(x,t)}+e^{\nu_c(x,t)}.

The network outputs the observed-state probabilities PP, not FkF_k; FkF_k is obtained from the AJ recursion below. Unified configuration: d=32d=32, L=1L=1 (about 2.4K parameters); time enters on its raw scale.

Loss: integrated cross-entropy over the observed state

In every minibatch, draw MM times ti1,…,tiM∼iidwt_{i1},\dots,t_{iM}\overset{\text{iid}}{\sim}w for each sample independently (independent of YiY_i) and label them by ηi(t)\eta_i(t):

ℓ(xi,Yi,Δi)=1M∑m=1M−logPηi(tim)(tim∣xi),\ell(x_i,Y_i,\Delta_i)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}-\log P_{\eta_i(t_{im})}(t_{im}\mid x_i),

−logPηi(t)(t∣xi)={−logP0(t∣xi),t<Yi,−logPk(t∣xi),t≥Yi,Δi=k≥1,−logPc(t∣xi),t≥Yi,Δi=0.-\log P_{\eta_i(t)}(t\mid x_i)=\begin{cases}-\log P_0(t\mid x_i), & t<Y_i,\\ -\log P_k(t\mid x_i), & t\ge Y_i,\ \Delta_i=k\ge1,\\ -\log P_c(t\mid x_i), & t\ge Y_i,\ \Delta_i=0.\end{cases}

The training objective is the sample average, i.e., a Monte Carlo approximation of the integral

minθℒ=1n∑i=1nℓ(xi,Yi,Δi)≈1n∑i=1n∫0τ−logPηi(t)(t∣xi)w(dt).\min_\theta\ \ ℒ=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell(x_i,Y_i,\Delta_i)\ \approx\ \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_0^\tau-\log P_{\eta_i(t)}(t\mid x_i)\,w(dt).

  • Time distribution ww: the empirical distribution of the training event times (YY with Δ>0\Delta>0), with M=4M=4. This aligns the training weight with the evaluation grid (event-time quantiles) and is not affected by administrative censoring points.
  • Compared with SoftComp (manuscript): the single-point cross-entropy at the observed time, time augmentation, the auxiliary Brier term, and class weights are all gone; the only new ingredient is the label for t≥Yit\ge Y_i (cause kk or censored cc).

Why GG is not needed: for every tt,

𝔼[−logPη(t)(t∣X)|X=x]=−∑l∈{0,1,…,K,c}Pl*(t∣x)logPl(t∣x)≥−∑lPl*(t∣x)logPl*(t∣x),\mathbb{E}\big[-\log P_{\eta(t)}(t\mid X)\,\big|\,X=x\big]=-\sum_{l\in\{0,1,\dots,K,c\}}P^{\ast}_l(t\mid x)\log P_l(t\mid x)\ \ge\ -\sum_{l}P^{\ast}_l(t\mid x)\log P^{\ast}_l(t\mid x),

with equality if and only if P=P*P=P^{\ast} (Gibbs' inequality). Hence, whatever ww and MM are, the minimizer is the true observed-state probability P*P^{\ast}; all labels are observed, so GG is never needed during training. Replacing the log score by the Brier score gives Munch & Gerds' B‾τ\bar B_\tau.

Recovering the CIF: the Aalen–Johansen identity

On a fine grid 0=s0<s1<…<sJ0=s_0<s_1<\dots<s_J, start from S(s0)=1S(s_0)=1 and Fk(s0)=0F_k(s_0)=0 and recurse:

ΔΛk(sj)=[Pk(sj)−Pk(sj−1)]+P0(sj−1),Fk(sj)=Fk(sj−1)+S(sj−1)ΔΛk(sj),S(sj)=S(sj−1)(1−∑kΔΛk(sj)).\begin{aligned} \Delta\Lambda_k(s_j)&=\frac{\left[P_k(s_j)-P_k(s_{j-1})\right]_+}{P_0(s_{j-1})},\\ F_k(s_j)&=F_k(s_{j-1})+S(s_{j-1})\,\Delta\Lambda_k(s_j),\\ S(s_j)&=S(s_{j-1})\left(1-\sum_{k}\Delta\Lambda_k(s_j)\right). \end{aligned}

This corresponds to Eqs. (3) and (11) of Munch & Gerds. Since dPk(t)=G(t−)dFk(t)dP_k(t)=G(t^-)\,dF_k(t) and P0(t−)=S(t−)G(t−)P_0(t^-)=S(t^-)\,G(t^-), we have dPk(t)/P0(t−)=dFk(t)/S(t−)=dΛk(t)dP_k(t)/P_0(t^-)=dF_k(t)/S(t^-)=d\Lambda_k(t), so GG cancels in the ratio. The resulting CIFs are monotone by construction and satisfy S+∑kFk=1S+\sum_kF_k=1, so neither PAV nor rescaling is needed. The recursion has two built-in safeguards: the increments ΔPk\Delta P_k are truncated at zero, and if ∑kΔΛk>1\sum_k\Delta\Lambda_k>1 in some step, the increments are rescaled to sum to one so that S≥0S\ge0.

Test 1: uncensored control

Thomas's first comment

“could you check if the performance boost is present in uncensored data too? if it vanishes there, I would guess that debiasing the loss functions from the censoring dependence will also remove the performance boost in censored data.”

He is asking two things:

  1. The literal question: is SoftComp's performance gain still there on data without censoring?
  2. The inference behind it: if the gain vanishes without censoring, it comes from the censoring dependence of the loss; debiasing the loss would then remove the gain on censored data as well.

“Performance gain” can mean SoftComp's advantage over the other methods, or the gain from time augmentation (which the manuscript credits with “dramatically improving discrimination”). Both readings are tested below.

The “debiasing” he refers to is exactly what JointSoftComp does

Analysis

  1. The gain persists without censoring. On uncensored data JointSoftComp is best in MSE and IBS (MSE: Case II 1.65 vs. 2.98 for NeuralFG; Case III 0.82 vs. 2.17) and best or tied for best in CtdC^{\text{td}}. Its advantage does not depend on censoring.
  2. Thomas's inference is directly refuted. JointSoftComp is exactly the debiased version he suggested. Under censoring, its MSE is about 1/4 (Case II: 2.10 vs. 8.20) and 1/3 (Case III: 1.13 vs. 3.20) of that of SoftComp (manuscript), its IBS is better, and CtdC^{\text{td}} is essentially unchanged; in Case III, SoftComp (manuscript) is 0.006 higher. Debiasing did not remove the gain; it increased it.
  3. SoftComp (manuscript) does depend on censoring. Removing censoring makes its MSE worse (Case II: 8.20 → 15.10; Case III: 3.20 → 4.30), while every other method stays the same or improves. This confirms Thomas's concern about its loss.
  4. The gain from time augmentation is even larger without censoring, but this reveals a flaw of the loss of SoftComp (manuscript). Without censoring, Eq. (5) gives cause labels only at event times and never an “at risk” label; its population target is the hazard share λk(t)/λ(t)\lambda_k(t)/\lambda(t), with S*≡0S^{\ast}\equiv0. Augmentation is then the only source of “at risk” information (without it, the MSE is 65.2 / 61.1): a patch on a loss that does not target the CIF.

Short answer: the gain is still there on uncensored data, and debiasing the loss as suggested does not remove it; it makes it larger.

Test 2: censoring levels relative to a fixed horizon

Thomas's second comment

“For the unit tests I would recommend to fix a maximum time horizon, say τ=5 years, and then to vary the censoring percentage relative to this fixed time horizon. it is ambiguous to say that there were ρ=20% censored without saying until what time. I would also recommend to include the uncensored case ρ=0%.”

Saying “ρ=20% censored” without stating up to which time is ambiguous. Instead, fix a maximum horizon τ, define the censoring percentage relative to τ, vary it with τ held fixed, and include the uncensored case ρ=0%. This is also the setting of Munch & Gerds' framework, in which everything is defined on a fixed prediction horizon [0,τ][0,\tau].

Defining the censoring percentage: old vs. new

Old definition (earlier report and first round of checks): C∼Exp(λc)C\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\lambda_c), calibrated so that P(C<medianT)=ρP(C<\mathrm{median}\,T)=\rho. This is neither the fraction of censored observations nor tied to any time point; Algorithm 1 of the manuscript instead states P(C<T)≈ρP(C<T)\approx\rho, which does not match the code either.

New definition (this test): fix τ=20\tau=20; censoring times are C∼Exp(λc)C\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\lambda_c) (with rate λce0.8x1\lambda_c e^{0.8x_1} for covariate-dependent censoring), and λc\lambda_c is calibrated so that

P{C<min(T,τ)}=ρ,Y=min(T,C,τ),Δ=ϵ𝟙{T≤min(C,τ)}.P\{C<\min(T,\tau)\}=\rho,\qquad Y=\min(T,C,\tau),\qquad \Delta=\epsilon\,𝟙\{T\le\min(C,\tau)\}.

That is, ρ is the fraction of subjects censored before τ, whose status at τ is therefore unknown. Subjects still event-free at τ are cut off at τ; their status at τ is known, and they do not count towards ρ. We use ρ = 0, 20%, 50%, 80%; at ρ=0 the data on [0,τ][0,\tau] are complete. Realized fractions censored before τ in the training sets: 0.000 / 0.200 / 0.503 / 0.801 (Case III) and 0.000 / 0.198 / 0.501 / 0.800 (constant hazards).

Analysis

  1. SoftComp (manuscript) is biased already at ρ=0, and the bias grows systematically with ρ. At t=20, the average bias in Case III goes from −0.10 (ρ=0) to −0.19 (ρ=80%), and for cause 1 under constant hazards from −0.15 to −0.33. This matches the population targets above: without censoring, the targets of Eq. (5) and of augmentation are still not the CIF.
  2. JointSoftComp is approximately unbiased for ρ ≤ 50%. The biases of all three causes are within about 0.01 (exceptions: causes 2 and 3 under constant hazards at ρ=50%, with 0.016 and −0.035); the MSE grows only mildly with censoring (Case III: 1.04 → 1.76), and the IBS is better in every setting.
  3. CtdC^{\text{td}} is similar for both. CtdC^{\text{td}} only looks at rankings and is insensitive to a common systematic shift, so the bias of SoftComp (manuscript) shows up mainly in MSE and IBS. The exception is covariate-dependent censoring at ρ=80%, where SoftComp (manuscript) drops to 0.594, versus 0.667 for JointSoftComp.
  4. At ρ=80% the error is mainly a lack of information. Only 0%–0.3% of the training subjects are still under observation at τ, so there are almost no data near τ. JointSoftComp's error also increases noticeably, but its MSE is still 3–5 times smaller than that of SoftComp (manuscript); in the no-covariate check, the maximum error of the nonparametric AJ estimate itself rises from 0.007 to 0.036. This is a limit of identifiability, not a bias of the loss (Munch & Gerds also assume that G(τ∣x)G(\tau\mid x) is bounded away from 0).

Short answer: the censoring percentage is now defined relative to a fixed τ, and ρ=0 is included. The loss of SoftComp (manuscript) is biased already at ρ=0 and drifts with ρ; JointSoftComp is approximately unbiased for ρ ≤ 50%, and its error at ρ=80% comes mainly from the lack of information near τ.
